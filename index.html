The Midnight Basket

A wicker basket floated through the nursery fog at quarter past midnight. Inside was Pip: tiny skull face, sleepy amber eyes, and a starry nightcap resting crooked. Pip yawned once, smiled at the moon, and made the nursery home.

The Stolen Star

Every evening at twilight, Pip climbs the tallest bedpost with a wooden ladder. One by one, Pip plucks falling stars from the windowsill and tucks them inside the nightcap's woolly brim to keep the bad dreams far away.

Mixing Lantern Honey

In the nursery pantry, Pip stirs glow-worm nectar, moon water, and warm honey in a bronze kettle. That is how the dream lanterns are filled: with light soft enough that even the fussiest little ghost can fall asleep.

Rooftop Wind Secrets

Clouds don't steer with wheels; they steer with whispers. When Pip delivers dreams over cobblestones, Pip blows softly across the chimney pots. The chimneys whistle back, guiding the cloud straight to every bedroom with an open window.

The Sock Detective

Where do lost socks go? They don't vanish. Pip collects single socks from the nursery floor and sews them into soft cloud hammocks for sleepy moths. Pip says everyone deserves cozy toes, even the tiniest night fliers.

Goodnight, Little Dreamer

The sun is still hours away, but Pip's eyes are growing heavy. The music box slows, the last lantern flickers low, and the cloud curls up on the rug. Close your eyes now. Pip will watch over your dreams until morning.

The hiccup

                
Nobody knows exactly where Pip came from. The night nurse found a laundry basket
                  on the nursery step, a nightcap two sizes too big, and one very loud hiccup. Inside the basket: Pip,
                  giggling at the moon.

              

            

            

              

                
              

              

                
No silence allowed

                
The Midnight Nursery had one rule: every little ghost falls asleep to something.
                  Humming, rain, a rusty fan. Pip took one look at all that waiting quiet and decided silence was a
                  problem to be engineered.

              

            

            

              

                
              

              

                
The first lullaby

                
Pip wound a dusty music box and turned the key the wrong way. Out came five
                  notes, slightly crooked, just like Pip's smile. Eleven ghosts yawned at once. Nobody has stayed up
                  past midnight since.

              

            

            

              

                
              

              

                
The night shift

                
Now Pip is a Junior Dream Engineer: lullabies in the lab, nightlights on the
                  shelf, dreams delivered by cloud before sunrise. Tonight's route has seven stops. Leave your window
                  open a crack.

              

            

            

              

                
              

              

                
A pocket of stars

                
One evening, a star fell into Pip's pocket. Pip climbed the cloud ladder, returned it to the sky, and
                  kept one tiny sparkle for the nursery window.

              

            

            

              

                
              

              

                
Goodnight, Pip

                
When the last window glows, Pip curls up beneath the stars. The nursery is quiet, the dreams are
                  safe, and tomorrow has room for another little wonder.
